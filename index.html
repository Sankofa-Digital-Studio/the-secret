It doesn’t have to be formal.

I was never very good at saying the important things while I was standing inside them.

Sometimes I joked. Sometimes I over-explained. Sometimes I went quiet and hoped you would somehow hear what I could not arrange properly into words.

What I was trying to say was simple: being seen by you mattered to me.

This is from me

I can only tell you what it meant to me.

I made this because knowing you left real things in my heart: gratitude, laughter, questions, prayer, lessons and care. It is not a case for or against either of us.

Where I remember something, I call it a memory. Where I interpret it, I call it my reflection. Where your heart is concerned, that answer remains yours.

But with you, it felt right.

I did not expect you to become a place where I could speak about myself. I had spent a long time learning how to keep difficult things contained.

Then our conversations became ordinary enough to feel safe: checking in, laughing, asking if the other person was okay, saying good morning, saying good night.

I think that is why the ordinary moments became so important to me.

Sometimes a story grows in ordinary repetition.

Not every moment arrived with fireworks. Some were greetings, jokes, check-ins and the simple rhythm of knowing I would hear from you again.

There was something about you when you wore your hair tied up like that.

I used to tease you and call you my princess. And, without missing a beat, you would correct me: “Queen.”

You were right.

Not because of a title, or because I was trying to flatter you, but because sometimes there was this quiet radiance about you that made everything around you seem a little less important.

Sometimes it was a morning message lighting up my phone before the day had properly begun. Sometimes it was a goodnight at the end of a long day. And sometimes it was nothing more than passing each other and catching that secret little smile — the kind that lasted only a second, but somehow followed me for hours afterwards.

Those moments were small enough that nobody else would have noticed them. I did.

I remember the way they warmed ordinary days. The way seeing your name could change the atmosphere of a morning. The way one smile across a room could feel like we had shared something that belonged only to us.

So yes, I called you princess. But you corrected me.

Queen.

And looking back, I think what stayed with me wasn't the crown I imagined on your head. It was the way your presence made ordinary moments feel royal.

Some memories arrive loudly. Others are only a glance, a message, a smile. And somehow those are the ones that become etched deepest into the heart.

Ice cream solves almost everything.

Places become memories because of who was there.

A drive, a meal, a stop for something sweet — small things can carry an entire day back with them.

I remember that day at Roxy’s.

The place itself probably looked completely ordinary to everyone else — a meal, some ice cream, another stop in another day.

But I remember leaving.

And then hearing you call me.

I turned around.

And you told me that you liked me too.

There are sentences that become important only after time has passed. And then there are some you somehow know, while they are being spoken, that you are going to carry for a very long time.

That was one of mine.

For a moment, everything else became background noise.

The cars. The restaurant. Whatever I had been thinking about a few seconds earlier.

All I really remember is turning back toward you and hearing those words.

Maybe that is why I remember the place so clearly.

Not because Roxy’s was extraordinary.

Because you were there, and for one small moment something I had been quietly carrying in my heart was spoken back to me.

The ice cream was almost as sweet as those words were to me.

Almost.

And whatever happened before or after that day, I will always be grateful that moment existed.

Some memories don't need to become promises about the future to remain precious.

Sometimes it is enough that, once upon a very ordinary day, I turned around…

and you told me you liked me too.

I will always hold onto that moment.

You are His workmanship.

What I saw was never only appearance.

Beauty was there, yes. But what stayed with me was the person — humour, stubbornness, faith, softness, contradictions, strength and all.

Make me understand.

I wanted to be heard. I also needed to learn how to listen better.

There were times I came to you carrying too much feeling at once. I wanted reassurance, clarity, closeness, and to know that what mattered deeply to me had been heard.

Let me say the rest

But wanting to be understood is not the same as already understanding you. I know there were moments when my fear made me speak from my own view before I had made enough room for yours.

What I wish I had said more clearly is: I was not asking you to surrender your experience to mine. I was asking whether we could sit beside each other long enough to see both.

It is okay to not be okay — and that's okay too.

Care was never supposed to require perfection.

You once gave me words I needed: that I was not a burden. I want that same gentleness returned here.
